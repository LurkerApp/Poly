What does this mean?

A plain-English guide to the numbers on this page

Expected Move
The range the options market thinks the stock will stay within by expiration. A move of ±$5 on a $500 stock means traders expect the price to stay between $495 and $505.
VIX — Fear Index
The VIX measures how much fear or uncertainty is in the market. Below 15 = calm. 15–25 = moderate uncertainty. Above 25 = elevated fear. Above 30 = high volatility.
IV vs HV Spread
Implied Volatility (IV) is what options traders expect. Historical Volatility (HV) is what actually happened. When IV > HV, options are expensive — sellers have an edge. When IV < HV, options are cheap — buyers have an edge.
Put/Call Ratio (bars)
The red/teal bars on the chart compare bearish bets (puts) vs bullish bets (calls) for each expiration. Above 1.0 = more bearish positioning. Below 1.0 = more bullish. The dotted line marks 1.0 (neutral).
DTE — Days to Expiry
How many days until the options contract expires. Shorter DTE = smaller expected move but faster time decay. Longer DTE = larger expected move with more time for the trade to play out.
ATM Straddle
We calculate the expected move by adding the At-The-Money call and put prices. This is the classic "straddle" — buying both sides. The combined price tells you how much the market expects to move.
