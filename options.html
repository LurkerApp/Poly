What does this mean?

Plain-English guide to the numbers on this page

Expected Move
The range the options market thinks the stock will stay within by expiration. A ±$5 move on a $500 stock means traders expect the price to stay between $495 and $505.
VIX — Fear Index
Measures market fear. Below 15 = calm. 15–25 = moderate. Above 25 = elevated fear. Above 30 = high volatility. All-time high was ~90 during 2008 crisis.
IV vs HV Spread
IV is what traders expect. HV is what actually happened. IV > HV means options are expensive — sellers have an edge. IV < HV means options are cheap — buyers have an edge.
Put/Call Ratio (bars below chart)
Red bars = more puts (bearish bets) than calls. Teal bars = more calls (bullish). Above 1.0 = bearish lean. Below 1.0 = bullish lean. The dotted line marks neutral (1.0).
OI Profile (right side of chart)
Horizontal bars show where options are concentrated by strike price. Wide bars = lots of open interest = strong price magnet. Teal = calls, red = puts, stacked to show total at each level.
Max Pain
The strike price where the most options expire worthless. Market makers profit most here so price tends to drift toward Max Pain as expiration approaches — especially in the final days.
